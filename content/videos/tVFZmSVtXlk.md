## The thesis

Every AI breakthrough has the same hidden enemy — not the chip, not the data, but the watt. A single AI server rack today pulls more power than an entire city block did a decade ago, and by 2030 that number will triple. The question nobody is asking is how you get that much electricity into something the size of a vending machine without it catching fire. Infineon Technologies (IFX) is the answer — the company standing at every single point where electricity changes form, from the grid all the way down to the GPU itself.

The market writes Infineon off as a German automotive chipmaker and moves on. That misread is the opportunity. While everyone obsesses over who controls the brain of AI, Infineon controls the nervous system.

## Key numbers

- **~€106.6B market cap** — among Europe's largest chipmakers, priced for a strong AI-driven recovery with a P/E TTM of ~94x reflecting trailing earnings still depressed from the recent downcycle
- **€3.81B Q2 FY26 revenue, +6% sequential** — sixth straight quarter of sequential growth as AI and auto demand firmed up; segment margin climbed to 17.1% from 14.5% the prior quarter
- **€21B order backlog** — continuing its growth last quarter even during a cautious broader semiconductor cycle; locked-in infrastructure supply, not speculative demand
- **€1.5B AI-specific revenue guided for FY26**, climbing to €2.5B by FY27 — supply-limited, not demand-capped; that is before the 800V architecture reaches full commercial scale
- **€500M capital reallocation** — shifted from lagging automotive high-voltage lines into AI data center power investment; management actively fast-tracking capital to the highest-growth segment

## The setup

The core of the thesis is the 800-volt DC architecture that Infineon is building alongside Nvidia. Today's racks run on 24-volt systems that lose too much energy as heat just moving electricity the last few feet to the chip. By 2030 a single rack is projected to hit 1 megawatt — enough to power roughly 1,000 homes. Standard 24V infrastructure cannot handle that. The 800V shift routes DC straight from the building's main power line to the server board, with the final step-down conversion happening inches from the GPU. Fewer conversion stages means less heat, lower cooling requirements, and more raw power reaching the wafer. This is the difference between an AI factory that scales and one that melts.

Infineon's moat is its multi-material approach: Silicon, Silicon Carbide (SiC), and Gallium Nitride (GaN) running side by side, each optimised for a different stage of that 800V journey. Their GaN switches run at near 1 million cycles per second, achieving above 98% efficiency per stage. No competitor matches that breadth. And it doesn't matter who wins the hardware race — Nvidia, AMD, or a custom architecture nobody has heard of yet — they all have to solve the physics of getting power from the wall to the wafer. Infineon builds every single piece of that puzzle.

## Risk factors

- **Auto high-voltage and China exposure:** E-mobility pricing pressure and a China slowdown have already triggered one analyst downgrade. The automotive segment faces near-term margin pressure while the EV cycle resets.
- **Valuation at ~94x P/E TTM:** Trailing earnings are still depressed from the recent downcycle, inflating the multiple. Any growth disappointment at this level gets punished hard.
- **Cyclical semiconductor exposure:** Despite the AI tailwind, Infineon is not immune to broader end-market softness in industrial and consumer segments.

## What to watch

The key near-term signal is the pace of 800V architecture adoption across hyperscaler build-outs — any acceleration in Nvidia or AMD deployments directly accelerates Infineon's power semiconductor revenue. The longer-term wildcard is the Solid-State Transformer initiative: if Infineon successfully moves from powering individual server racks to controlling the substations feeding entire data centers, the addressable market expands by an order of magnitude. The company isn't betting on which AI company wins the race — it's betting that whoever wins will still need to plug in.
