## The thesis

Everyone is trading the chip. Almost nobody is pricing the heat coming off it — and when a modern accelerator runs out of somewhere to put that heat, it doesn't fail, it throttles: a hundred-thousand-dollar processor deliberately making itself slower because there was nowhere for the heat to go. Modine is a radiator company founded in Racine, Wisconsin in 1916 that bought a British mission-critical cooling specialist called Airedale in 2005 — long before anyone said "AI data center" out loud — and is now shedding its century-old automotive business to become a pure-play cooling company. The tell isn't the story. It's that one of the largest technology companies on Earth paid nine figures in cash, up front, just to hold a place in the production queue.

## Key numbers

- **$4B long-term capacity agreement, running through 2029** — signed with a single unnamed strategic data center customer for Airedale cooling equipment. Not a framework with a ceiling shared across a dozen vendors: one buyer, one supplier, multi-year revenue already contracted
- **$165M paid up front** — cash handed over ahead of delivery purely to fund the capacity to build the order. Prepayment is what a buyer does when the queue matters more than the price, and Modine still won't say who it is
- **35% of sales now come from data centers, up from 25% a year ago** — against FY26 revenue of $3.18B, a record $954.4M Q4, and $471.0M of adjusted EBITDA
- **FY27 guidance: net sales +20–35%, adjusted EBITDA +38–44%** — and that's before whatever the next hyperscale agreement adds
- **~11% of sales from one customer, 49% from the top ten** — the concentration that makes this story dramatic is also the risk sitting underneath it

## The setup

The moat isn't a patent, it's a liability record. Cooling a car is a tolerance problem; cooling a hyperscale campus is a trust problem — a room that overheats can take down a bank's trading system or a nine-figure training run silently, in a building nobody is watching in person. Hyperscalers buy that on twenty years of not being the reason a rack went dark, which is not something a well-funded newcomer can manufacture in eighteen months. Meanwhile the physics moved Modine's way: chip density passed the point where blowing air across a rack is enough, and direct-to-chip liquid cooling became the requirement rather than the upgrade — coolant distribution units and chillers Modine is shipping onto campuses now, not concept renders. All of that was being signed while the stock round-tripped hard off its highs on sentiment rather than orders, with a 52-week range of $111.18–$323.25 and roughly 71x trailing earnings at the time of recording.

## Risk factors

- **Component shortages** from supply-chain partners hit production in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026 and are tempering the very ramp-up Modine is racing to deliver on — the binding constraint can move from customer demand to Modine's own line
- **Customer concentration** — approximately 11% of sales from a single global technology customer and 49% from the top ten. If one hyperscaler blinks, a meaningful chunk of the growth blinks with it
- **The multiple carries no slack** — around 71x trailing earnings at recording, which prices in the guided growth actually arriving on schedule
- **The spin-off is still a plan, not a structure** — the Reverse Morris Trust separating the automotive business into Gentherm is targeted to close by the end of calendar 2026. Until it does, "pure-play cooling company" is an intention rather than a reported segment

## What to watch

Three checkpoints. The spin-off closing by the end of calendar 2026 is the one that changes what the company actually is — after it there are no truck parts and no tractors, just cooling. The new production lines coming online across multiple facilities this year are the test of whether the ramp can keep pace with orders already signed, and whether the component shortages have eased. And the thing that would confirm the chokepoint outright is a second prepayment — another customer deciding that reserving capacity is worth cash before delivery.
