## The thesis

Astera Labs doesn't build the GPU, and it doesn't build the rack that holds it. It builds the retimer and switch silicon that keeps every signal between a hundred thousand GPUs clean enough to still make sense on arrival instead of degrading into noise — the physical ceiling on how big an AI supercomputer can actually get. That single-purpose focus has put its parts on nearly 90% of the world's AI servers, and the company just posted a quarter that more than doubled revenue year-over-year while pulling its own product roadmap forward. The tension investors are weighing: is the market pricing this correctly, or right to be nervous about how much of that growth rides on a handful of customers staying loyal?

## Key numbers

- **$392.4M Q2 2026 revenue, +104% YoY, +27% sequentially** — a record quarter, beating Wall Street's estimate by more than $30M
- **$0.80 non-GAAP EPS** vs. a $0.69 estimate, with non-GAAP gross margin holding near 73%
- **$540-560M Q3 2026 guidance** — another ~38% sequential jump on an already-record quarter; Scorpio fabric switches are set to become Astera's largest product family a full quarter ahead of the company's own original schedule
- **One Scorpio switch handles up to 320 lanes** of simultaneous GPU traffic, routing and managing conversation across hundreds of GPUs inside a single AI fabric
- **Customer concentration:** one hyperscaler accounts for more than 70% of revenue; the top three customers together make up close to 86%

## The setup

There's no chart pattern driving this one — it's a fundamentals story trading at one of the richest multiples in the entire chip sector, a trailing P/E north of 200x, and now more than 250% above its 52-week low after the post-earnings pop of over 12% in a single session. The re-rating case rests on Astera's role only getting more essential with each hardware cycle: the next PCIe standard, generation seven, is already on the roadmap and doubles speeds again, which only deepens the signal-integrity problem Astera was built to solve. Astera is also a founding backer of UALink, the open industry standard supported by AMD, Google, and Microsoft, built as an alternative to Nvidia's proprietary NVLink — a hedge that lets its role in AI racks survive even if the industry stops standardizing around one company's chips. The company has also deepened its footprint in Taiwan, embedding engineers closer to the design teams at AMD, Arm, Intel, and Nvidia who spec next-generation AI racks before they're built, and this summer it joined the Nasdaq-100 — a signal Wall Street now treats this as core AI infrastructure, not a niche component vendor.

## Risk factors

- **Customer concentration:** a single hyperscaler is more than 70% of revenue, and the top three make up ~86% — if any one of them decided to build connectivity in-house, the growth story changes fast
- **Valuation risk:** a trailing P/E north of 200x prices in years of continued hypergrowth; any stumble compresses a multiple with no earnings cushion beneath it
- **Competitive risk:** Broadcom and Marvell are both trying to build competing solutions; Astera's counter is that its moat isn't the silicon alone but the COSMOS software layer, which gives data center operators a live map of every cable and connection across a cluster and flags degradation before failure

## What to watch

Q3 2026 results are the next checkpoint — guided to $540-560M, with Scorpio switches expected to become Astera's largest product family a full quarter ahead of the original plan. Watch whether that ramp keeps outpacing its own roadmap, and whether any of the three customers responsible for roughly 86% of revenue show signs of moving connectivity in-house. The Gen7 PCIe rollout and continued UALink adoption are the multi-year catalysts the case leans on to argue Astera's role only gets more essential from here, not less.
