## The thesis

Karman doesn't build a missile, a rocket, or a torpedo system of its own — it builds the parts that have to survive the violence of launch and reentry: payload fairings, interstage structures, propulsion components, and the thermal-protection ceramics that go inside almost everyone else's system. That's the wall a competitor can't undercut on price alone — every part carries decades of qualification testing and flight heritage, and switching suppliers means re-running that entire process on the government's clock. Record growth, a raised guide, and more than $2B in combined backlog and new demand commitments landed in a single month, and the stock sold off anyway — purely on leverage and valuation, not on the underlying business slowing down.

## Key numbers

- **Q1 FY2026 revenue:** $151.2M, up 51% year-over-year — a record for the company, with net income flipping from a $4.8M loss a year earlier to a $7.8M profit
- **Backlog:** just over $1B, up 61% in a single year — plus more than another $1B in newly disclosed contingent multi-year demand commitments from four separate space and defense customers
- **Margins:** adjusted EBITDA margin near 30%, gross margin expanded to 42%
- **FY2026 guidance:** raised to $720M–$735M, roughly 54% growth on top of the prior year — full-year 2025 revenue was already up 36.6%
- **Debt:** roughly $758M taken on, tied to short-term interest benchmarks, to fund acquisitions in maritime defense and precision engineering

## The setup

The stock trades more than 50% below its 52-week high even after a record quarter, a raised full-year guide, and over a billion dollars in new demand commitments disclosed in a single month — a valuation the market has gotten skittish about given the debt load, not a sign the business is slowing. In July, Karman agreed to acquire Walker Precision Engineering, a Glasgow-based precision manufacturer, for roughly $94M — its first move into international production — and Northrop Grumman awarded Karman a contract on the U.S. Navy's MK 54 torpedo program, proof the maritime expansion is already converting into real, named work. Karman also joined the S&P SmallCap 600 in July, and behind all of it sits the Golden Dome missile defense initiative — a program expected to run decades and cost north of half a trillion dollars over its lifetime, squarely inside the hypersonics and missile-defense markets Karman already supplies.

## Risk factors

- Roughly $758M in debt, tied to short-term interest benchmarks, taken on to fund the acquisition strategy
- A valuation the market has clearly gotten nervous about — the stock is down more than 50% from its high despite improving fundamentals, a real risk when a stock carries both real debt and a rich multiple
- Growth is partly acquisition-fueled (Walker Precision, maritime and precision-engineering deals) rather than purely organic

## What to watch

Whether the newly disclosed $1B+ in contingent multi-year demand commitments converts into firm backlog, and whether the Walker Precision and MK 54 torpedo wins keep proving the maritime-defense expansion is real rather than just acquisition press releases. Longer-term, the Golden Dome missile defense initiative is the multi-decade tailwind sitting behind the entire hypersonics and missile-defense book.
