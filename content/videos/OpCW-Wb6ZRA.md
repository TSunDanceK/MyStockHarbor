## The thesis

The market is treating AI like a software revolution. It isn't. It is an industrial, heavy-hardware bottleneck. While a handful of tech platforms capture 99% of media attention, a tiny group of deep-value infrastructure providers control 100% of the physical reality. The first structural barrier to the AI boom isn't a lack of data — it is a critical, systemic deficit of electricity. In major global tech hubs, vacant data center capacity has fallen below 3%, entirely because data centers cannot secure agreements to connect to the main electrical grid. The grid is full.

GE Vernova is the company that solves this. When an enterprise cloud provider can't wait five years for a local utility connection, they bypass the grid entirely and build custom, on-site industrial generation plants next to the server farm. GEV dominates the manufacturing of ultra-high-capacity gas turbines and grid systems that make that possible.

- **$44.5B–$45.5B full-year revenue guidance** — management guiding up, not managing down

- **The dependency chain:** Big Tech → Nvidia/AMD → TSMC → physical power, cooling, and grid infrastructure; GEV sits at the foundational layer where the entire stack begins

## The setup

The physical dependency chain runs deeper than most investors realise. Big Tech spends billions securing chips from Nvidia and AMD. Nvidia designs chips but doesn't manufacture them — that sits with TSMC. But even a perfect wafer can't function without massive physical infrastructure underneath it: power generation, cooling, and grid connectivity. GEV operates at that foundational layer. You cannot boot up a data center without generating the electricity first.

The moat is industrial, not algorithmic. While software platforms are locked in a speculative race for user adoption, GEV operates with unshakeable pricing power: every AI supercluster that bypasses the municipal grid needs GEV's gas turbines and power hardware to go live. That demand is not discretionary and it is not cyclical in the short term — it is a physical prerequisite.

## Risk factors

- **Logistics and supply chain fragility:** If GEV encounters a major deployment delay — materials, manufacturing capacity, or installation bottlenecks — data center build-outs delay by months or years, with a direct domino effect on Nvidia chip shipments and Big Tech capex deployment.
- **Grid dependency creates concentration risk:** The thesis relies on hyperscalers continuing to bypass municipal utilities. If grid expansion accelerates faster than expected, the urgency driving on-site generation demand softens.
- **Industrial business operates on long cycles:** GEV's backlog is enormous but conversion to revenue takes time. A macro slowdown compresses near-term earnings even if the long-term order book remains intact.
- **Valuation reflects the narrative:** At these levels the market is already pricing in significant execution on the backlog. Any miss on conversion or margin guidance gets punished at an industrial-scale multiple.

## What to watch

The clearest signal is backlog conversion rate — specifically how fast GEV is translating its $163B order book into deployed, revenue-generating hardware. Watch quarterly EBITDA margin progression as a gauge of pricing power holding through the delivery cycle. The macro wildcard is grid expansion: if major utilities accelerate capacity additions and reduce the wait time for grid connections, the urgency behind on-site generation softens and GEV's pricing power with it. Follow the power lines, not the software multiples.
