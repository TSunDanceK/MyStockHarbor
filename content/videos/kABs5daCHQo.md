## The thesis

AeroVironment has lost more than 60% of its value from its late-2025 highs after a Space Force contract worth up to $1.7 billion unravelled in three stages — a stop work order in January, a recompete announcement in March, and a full termination confirmed alongside a $151.3 million goodwill impairment. The stock is now pressing near multi-year lows with the RSI deeply oversold, and two near-term catalysts — Q4 earnings on 29 June and an investor day on 8 July — set up a binary moment: either management demonstrates the core drone business is intact and growing, or the selloff continues into price discovery.

The core argument for watching AVAV here is simple: the SCAR/BADGER debacle was a space segment problem, not an autonomous systems problem. The Switchblade loitering munition franchise, the BlueHalo integration, and the $1.1 billion funded backlog are all separate from the contract that failed. The question is whether the market is pricing the whole company at the wrong discount.

## Key numbers

- **Q3 FY26 revenue:** $408M, up 143% year-over-year — but that growth was largely BlueHalo acquisition-driven, not organic
- **Non-GAAP EPS:** $0.64, missing the $0.68–$0.72 consensus
- **Funded backlog:** $1.1B as of 31 January 2026, a record — driven by Switchblade demand and the BlueHalo integration
- **FY26 revenue guidance:** $1.85B–$1.95B (narrowed from $1.90B–$2.00B after the SCAR write-down)
- **BADGER/SCAR contract value:** ~$1.7B — terminated for convenience by the Space Force, triggering a recompete process
- **Goodwill impairment:** $151.3M in Q3, causing a GAAP net loss of $156.6M on a one-time charge

## The setup

At the time of the video (21 June 2026), AVAV was trading around $163 — near its lowest level in several years and pressing against the lower boundary of any meaningful technical support. The stock had failed three times to reclaim the $200 area after the March Q3 report, and each attempted recovery had been met with fresh selling pressure from the ongoing class action lawsuit headlines. The RSI was in the mid-to-low 30s on the daily chart, technically in oversold territory but with no clear sign of demand absorption yet. The key level to watch was the $155–$160 zone; a sustained break below that with volume would have opened up a move with no obvious chart support until the $100–$120 area.

## Risk factors

- **Class action litigation:** Multiple law firms filed suits covering the period June 2025–March 2026, alleging AVAV misled investors about SCAR. The lead plaintiff deadline is 27 July 2026 — this overhang is likely to keep institutional buyers cautious near-term.
- **Financial restatement:** AV restated Q3 results after an error in goodwill valuation tied to deferred tax items, increasing the net loss by ~$87M. This eroded confidence in the CFO transition (new CFO Sean Woodward took over May 2026).
- **Space segment permanently impaired:** The BADGER program is gone. Even if AV wins the SCAR recompete, it will be on far less favourable terms with multiple vendors competing. The space revenue line is not recovering to pre-2026 levels.
- **Execution risk on record Q4:** Management guided for record Q4 revenue. Missing that, or issuing soft FY27 guidance at the investor day, would be a second body blow with the stock already at multi-year lows.

## What to watch

The two events that matter most are Q4 earnings on 29 June 2026 and the investor day on 8 July 2026. If Q4 revenue hits or exceeds guidance and management provides constructive FY27 guidance that demonstrates the core autonomous systems business is growing independently of the SCAR contract, the stock has significant room to re-rate given that 15 of 15 analysts covering it still have buy ratings with an average target above $300. If either event disappoints, or if the litigation escalates materially before then, the $155 support becomes the line in the sand — and below it there is very little technical structure to slow a move lower.
