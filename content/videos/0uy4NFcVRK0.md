## The thesis

What if a forty-five-minute crawl through traffic took about ten minutes instead, in an aircraft so quiet the people below might barely notice it? Joby Aviation's electric air taxi is already flying: in New York City, and across the desert outside Dubai. Delta, Toyota and Uber have all put money behind it.

And yet the stock is down around 70% in a year. The question the video asks is not whether the aircraft can fly. It already does. The question is whether the business can stay in the air long enough to carry paying passengers.

## Key numbers

- **About $5.95 a share when the video was made** (2 Oct 2026), down roughly 70% from the 52-week high of **$19.98** (7 Oct 2025).
- **$38.6M Q2 2026 revenue**, of which **$36.2M came from Blade**, the helicopter and charter business Joby bought last year. Air taxi revenue has not started.
- **~51x price-to-sales**, on about $116M of trailing revenue, almost all of it helicopter charter.
- **-$245M Q2 2026 net loss**, with an adjusted EBITDA loss of about $197M in the quarter.
- **~$2.3B in cash and short-term investments** at 30 June. About $450M of that is set aside to buy Resonant Sciences, a defence-tech company, in a deal expected to close next year.
- **$202M of cash used in Q2 2026.** Guidance for the second half of 2026 is $385M to $415M.
- **$1.2B+ raised in January** through new shares and convertible bonds, which diluted existing shareholders.

## The setup

Founder JoeBen Bevirt grew up in an off-grid community deep in the redwoods north of Santa Cruz, with a very long trip to school, and started dreaming of aircraft that could lift straight off the ground. After co-founding a lab-robotics company later bought by Agilent and the company behind the GorillaPod tripod, he founded Joby in 2009. It worked mostly in secret for about a decade, built the electric motors for NASA's X-57, and drew a Toyota-led $590 million round in 2020. Uber handed over its Uber Elevate division later that year, and Joby listed on the New York Stock Exchange in 2021.

The aircraft has six tilting electric propellers. They lift it off like a helicopter, then tilt forward to fly like a plane, carrying a pilot and four passengers at up to 200 miles an hour. In NASA's 2021 tests, measured flying over at about 1,640 feet at 100 knots, it registered 45.2 decibels, roughly a quiet library. On takeoff and landing, measured about 330 feet away, it stayed under 65 decibels, which Joby compares to a normal conversation. Joby's modelling of a downtown Los Angeles to John Wayne Airport trip puts the area where its aircraft is louder than background noise at about a sixth of a square mile for the round trip, against about 45 square miles for a helicopter. Battery work is led by Jon Wagner, formerly head of battery engineering at Tesla, and one aircraft flew 154 miles on a single charge in 2021.

Demand is lining up. Delta invested $60 million in 2022, with up to $200 million possible, for home-to-airport flights starting in New York and Los Angeles. In Dubai, rides are set to be booked through the Uber app as "Uber Air", under a six-year exclusive right to run air taxis in the city. Joby estimates a trip from Dubai International Airport to Palm Jumeirah might take about ten minutes in the air, against around forty-five by car. Dubai's transport authority says the long-term aim is a fare close to an ordinary Uber or Careem ride.

On certification, Joby has completed four of the FAA's five stages. The fifth, the testing and proof, is about 20% complete by Joby's count and about 10% by the FAA's. Toyota holds a majority stake in a new manufacturing joint venture, and a large factory near Dayton, Ohio is lined up, with a stated target of four aircraft a month in 2027. Competitors have hit setbacks: Vertical Aerospace has pushed its certification out to 2029, and Boeing-owned Wisk is being sold to rival Archer.

## Risk factors

- **Cash burn:** Joby is spending roughly $200 million a quarter, and its air taxi has not yet carried a paying passenger.
- **Dilution:** January's $1.2B+ raise diluted shareholders, and any delay might mean more.
- **Certification:** the final FAA stage still has a long way to go, by Joby's count and especially by the FAA's.
- **Middle East timing:** after conflict in the Gulf disrupted regional air travel in late February, some analysts suggested the Dubai and Saudi plans might be delayed, and the stock fell about 18% in March during a wider sell-off. Joby says its Dubai partners remain committed.
- **Valuation:** at around 51 times sales, on revenue that is almost all helicopter charter, the price may already assume a great deal of future air taxi business.

## What to watch

- **First passengers:** the vertiport at Dubai International Airport is finished, and Joby has said it is aiming to carry its first passengers this year, in Dubai or the US. If that happens, it might be the moment this becomes a business story rather than a prototype story.
- **FAA for-credit test flights:** Joby said in March it expected FAA pilots to begin the official test flights that count towards final certification later this year. September's first US pilot-programme flights around Dallas–Fort Worth, without passengers, were a step along the way.
- **Cash runway:** second-half cash use against the $385M to $415M guidance, and whether the Resonant Sciences deal closes as planned.

Retail sees a stock down 70%. Toyota, Delta and Uber see an aircraft they have each put money behind. Whether the business can stay in the air long enough to land the founder's dream is the story to follow. This is not financial advice.
