## The thesis

Somewhere in America right now, a power plant is being built for a customer that doesn't exist yet — chips that haven't shipped, models that haven't finished training. Multiply that by hundreds of sites and you get hundreds of billions of dollars committed to infrastructure most people will never see, for a technology most people don't fully understand. Michael Burry — the investor who saw the 2008 housing crash coming before almost anyone else believed him — looked at that number and made a very different bet. Starting last November, he placed over a billion dollars against two of the biggest names in AI: Nvidia and Palantir.

For months, part of that bet has been winning. Palantir and Nvidia have both sold off hard, and headlines have called it the end of the AI trade. But there's a receipt sitting in a filing cabinet that none of those headlines mention: a single monthly check for $920 million, for computing power that's already been rented, installed, and paid for. This isn't really Burry against the market. It's a paper bet against a physical one — and the next ten days, with two earnings reports and a major share unlock all landing in quick succession, will show which version of the story the numbers actually support.

## Key numbers

- **$900M+ in Palantir short exposure, $187M in Nvidia puts** — public filings show Burry built both positions starting last November; his Nvidia puts (struck at $110, expiring December 2027) are currently underwater as chip demand has outpaced the bet against it
- **$920M/month for 110,000 GPUs** — what Google is paying to rent compute from SpaceX's Colossus cluster (built to train xAI's Grok), rather than build the capacity itself
- **$1.25B/month for 325,000 GPUs** — Anthropic's even larger version of the same compute-rental deal with SpaceX; together, the two contracts could bring in close to $26 billion a year
- **$500B+ in Nvidia chip orders** — backlog stretching to the end of this year, with Microsoft, Amazon, and Alphabet alone carrying AI infrastructure backlogs above $600 billion combined
- **85% revenue growth, 104% U.S. revenue growth** — Palantir's most recent quarter, its fastest as a public company, with net dollar retention on existing accounts up to 150% from 139% a year earlier
- **7 of 10** — the rough share of Michael Burry's major public calls since 2017 that, by one count, haven't panned out. The 2008 call was real. So is the messier track record since

## The setup

The case against Burry's bet starts with a fact that reframes the whole story: Google — one of the largest cloud computing companies that has ever existed — doesn't have enough computing power. So this year, it agreed to rent it, not from Amazon or Microsoft, but from SpaceX. Anthropic signed an even larger version of the same arrangement. Neither company is renting compute because it's cheap or convenient — they're doing it because you cannot build a data center or get a new power substation energized overnight, and whoever has spare capacity right now gets to charge what the market will bear. That's the real story behind the AI spending numbers: not hype, physical scarcity.

Nvidia's backlog and the hyperscalers' own committed spending back that scarcity thesis up directly, which is part of why Burry's Nvidia puts are currently losing money — demand has kept growing faster than the bet against it. Palantir's case is similar but more visible: the stock has fallen sharply since November even as the underlying business has accelerated, not slowed, with revenue growth, U.S. growth, and customer retention all climbing in the same quarter the stock was falling. SpaceX sits underneath both stories as the quiet infrastructure layer — a rocket company that built a compute business now generating more contracted revenue than its core launch business used to bring in on its own, while absorbing real dilution from its Cursor acquisition and real execution risk from back-to-back rough Starship tests.

## Risk factors

- **Burry's own track record:** roughly seven in ten of his major public calls since 2017 haven't worked out — the 2008 call built the legend, but it isn't the norm
- **Demand could still be pulled forward:** hyperscaler backlogs and compute-rental contracts assume today's AI spending pace holds; any slowdown in model training demand would hit Nvidia, Palantir, and SpaceX's compute business simultaneously
- **SpaceX-specific execution risk:** the Cursor acquisition diluted shareholders, and two consecutive rough Starship tests (including a booster failure on landing) have raised real questions about execution outside the compute business
- **Share supply risk:** a large SpaceX lockup expiration lands within days of this analysis, materially increasing the number of shares available to trade at a moment when the stock is already under pressure
- **Options decay:** Burry's Nvidia puts have a fixed expiration (December 2027) — being early on a thesis and being right in time for the position to pay off are not the same thing

## What to watch

Palantir reports earnings August 3rd — the first real test of whether 85% revenue growth and rising retention continue, or whether the market's recent skepticism was reading something real. SpaceX reports earnings August 4th, the first look at how the Google and Anthropic compute contracts are actually flowing through the company's numbers. Two days after that, on August 6th, roughly a billion SpaceX shares come off lockup — potentially quadrupling available float — and analysts are already split on whether that unlock triggers another leg down or whether the recent slide already priced it in. Somewhere in the middle of all of it, Burry's next 13F disclosure — whenever it lands — will show whether he's still holding these positions or has already moved on to the next bet.
