## The thesis

Every major technology cycle has a hidden choke point. During the internet revolution, wealth flowed to the companies printing standard silicon wafers. As we transition into a high-voltage world — driven by the double demand of the AI revolution and the massive EV shift — standard silicon is hitting a hard physical limit. It cannot handle extreme electrical loads without leaking heat and wasting energy. The future of energy management depends on one clear replacement: Silicon Carbide. And a single, highly specialised infrastructure play controls the foundation of that market.

Wolfspeed ($WOLF) is currently penalised by public markets for the near-term costs of heavy industrial scaling, while quietly building a dominant position beneath the global energy transition.

## Key numbers

- **~$150M quarterly revenue** — steady demand for power modules, though near-term EV sector fluctuations have slowed the initial volume ramp
- **-27% GAAP gross margin** — heavily impacted by start-up and under-utilisation costs as Mohawk Valley comes online; not reflective of normalised economics
- **~$1.2B cash and short-term investments** — strategic liquidity cushion funding advanced engineering lines without toxic debt loads
- **$140M–$160M next-quarter guidance** — core profitability depends on unlocking full utilisation at the automated production lines
- **200mm wafers vs legacy 150mm** — larger wafers yield significantly more chips per run, drastically cutting production costs and forcing competitors onto a different economic playbook

## The setup

Silicon Carbide's advantage over standard silicon is physical, not incremental. SiC features a wider bandgap that allows it to handle voltages 10 times higher and operational temperatures 3 times greater than standard silicon, while shrinking energy losses by up to 80%. This isn't a minor hardware upgrade — it is the fundamental infrastructure change required to keep high-capacity EV batteries and 1-megawatt AI server racks from melting under their own power demands.

The centre of Wolfspeed's long-term moat is the Mohawk Valley Fab in New York — the world's first and largest fully automated 200mm Silicon Carbide fabrication plant. Growing a single crystal boule of SiC takes weeks of hyper-controlled thermodynamic processing under extreme temperatures, where microscopic flaws can ruin an entire production batch. That manufacturing complexity is the moat. By scaling to 200mm Wolfspeed is establishing a cost structure legacy competitors running older 150mm technology simply cannot match. The company is an infrastructure pioneer navigating a capital-intensive transition — the short-term financial pain is the price of building a dominant position that would take competitors years and billions to replicate.

## Risk factors

- **Utilisation is the critical variable:** The -27% gross margin normalises only when Mohawk Valley reaches full utilisation. Any delay to that ramp — whether from EV softness, demand timing, or manufacturing yield issues — extends the loss period.
- **EV cycle timing risk:** Near-term EV sector fluctuations have already slowed the initial volume ramp. If the EV transition takes longer than expected to accelerate, Wolfspeed's largest demand driver underperforms.
- **The domino effect of a foundry stall:** If SiC material throughput drops, Infineon, Nvidia, and AMD power module lines delay, next-generation EV inverters and AI power racks cannot deploy, and the infrastructure wall hits multiple sectors simultaneously.
- **Capital intensity and liquidity:** $1.2B in cash provides a runway but not an unlimited one. If Mohawk Valley ramp takes longer than projected, the company may need to raise capital on unfavourable terms.

## What to watch

The single most important metric is Mohawk Valley utilisation rate — each quarter's progression toward full utilisation is the clearest signal that the normalised margin thesis is on track. Watch for volume commitment announcements from Tier 1 automotive OEMs and AI power module customers, since those locked-in orders de-risk the ramp timeline. The broader macro catalyst is the pace of the 800V EV architecture rollout and the 1-megawatt AI rack deployment cycle — both are direct, non-negotiable demand drivers for SiC that the underlying physics make inevitable. The transition to high-voltage automation is non-negotiable. Follow the high-voltage lines.
