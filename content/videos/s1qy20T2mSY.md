## The thesis

Every time you ask an AI a question, your words briefly stop being electricity. Inside the data center they are turned into light: laser pulses fired down glass fibre from one rack of chips to the next, so tens of thousands of GPUs can work as one machine. The chips and the power plants get the headlines. Far fewer investors ask where that light comes from.

It comes from lasers grown on indium phosphide (InP) wafers. Only a handful of companies can make those wafers at scale, and AXT Inc is one of them. The stock went from about $4 to above $140 in little more than a year, and it has since been cut roughly in half. Most people think AI's bottleneck is the chips. They may be looking in the wrong place.

## Key numbers

- **$47.6M Q2 2026 revenue** — up 164% year over year and 77% on the prior quarter.
- **~45% gross margin** — up from about 8% a year earlier as the indium phosphide mix rose. The company swung from a $7M loss to an $11M profit.
- **~$66M Q3 2026 revenue guidance** — results are due on October 29.
- **$100M+ InP order backlog** — extending into 2027. Management says it is not taking orders beyond what its capacity can support.
- **~80% share** — by industry estimates, AXT and Japan's Sumitomo together make nearly 80% of the world's indium phosphide wafers.
- **$87M in Lumentum deposits** — under a supply agreement that runs to the end of 2031.
- **$22.3M Coherent prepayment** — another major laser maker paying ahead to reserve supply.
- **$64.25 per share** — the price of April's share sale, which raised more than $600M (about $632M including the over-allotment).
- **$749M in cash and investments** — boosted by that share sale.

## The setup

AXT was founded in 1986 in Fremont, California, and is listed on the Nasdaq. For most of its history it was a small, cyclical supplier of crystal wafers: gallium arsenide, germanium and indium phosphide. Revenue rose and fell with the telecom cycle, and the stock sat in single digits for years. One detail matters a great deal: AXT is an American company, but its crystals are grown in China, through its subsidiary Tongmei.

The physics explains why AI changed the picture. Silicon is excellent at thinking but poor at making light; most of its energy escapes as heat. Indium phosphide gives energy back directly as light, at the wavelengths where glass fibre is most transparent. That is why the lasers inside the optical transceivers in AI data centres are built on InP. Silicon is the brain, indium phosphide is the voice, and the wafer is the soil the laser grows in.

Making that soil is slow. AXT uses vertical gradient freeze: molten material is cooled very slowly in a vertical crucible until a single crystal forms, and one defect can ruin a batch. Demand, meanwhile, is accelerating. Data centres are moving from 800G to 1.6T links, which need more lasers. The industry is also shifting to larger six-inch wafers. Six-inch InP wafer prices have reportedly risen about 250% since early 2025, to roughly $5,000 each.

Then there is the twist. China produces around 70% of the world's indium, and in February 2025 Beijing added indium phosphide to its export-control list. Since then, every order AXT ships outside China has needed its own export permit. The gatekeeper has a gatekeeper of its own.

The response has been to spend. Customers are prepaying to hold a place in line, and AXT is using the April raise to expand capacity. Management plans to take InP capacity from about $20M a quarter to around $60M by the end of 2026. It then aims for about $130M a quarter in 2027. Existing shareholders were diluted to fund capacity for demand that has not shipped yet.

## Risk factors

- **China export permits:** every shipment leaving China needs a permit. Management has said it cannot predict permit timing for any specific customer, and a slowdown might hit revenue directly.
- **Valuation:** on trailing earnings the P/E sits in the region of 1,000 (the datasheet shows 1,198; different sites use different trailing windows). The price may already assume several years of growth.
- **Volatility:** the stock peaked above $140 in late May and now trades in the mid-$70s. Leveraged ETFs on AXTI exist in both directions, which may amplify swings.
- **Dilution:** April's share sale enlarged the share count, and further raises are possible if expansion costs rise.
- **Insider selling:** insiders sold about $53M of stock over the past 12 months.
- **Execution:** tripling, then more than doubling, a crystal-growing operation is hard. Yield problems or delays might leave the backlog unfilled.
- **Concentration:** a small number of large optical customers account for much of the demand.

## What to watch

- **Q3 earnings on October 29:** whether the ~$66M guidance held, and whether export permits kept flowing through the summer.
- **Capacity ramp:** progress toward roughly $60M a quarter of InP capacity by the end of 2026.
- **Tongmei listing:** the Chinese subsidiary has shifted its planned listing from Shanghai to Hong Kong, on a timeline of roughly a year. How that unfolds may change how investors value the China side of the business.
- **S&P SmallCap 600:** AXT joined the index before the open on September 21, which puts the stock into a new set of index funds.

The business, the physics and the prepayments all look real. Customers are writing cheques that run to 2031 while the share price whipsaws, and every shipment still depends on a permit from Beijing. That tension is the story to follow. This is not financial advice.
