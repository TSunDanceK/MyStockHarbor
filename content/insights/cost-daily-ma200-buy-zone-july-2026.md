## What happened

Costco has slid about 16% from its 52-week high of $1,096.50 and is now trading right into its daily 200-day moving average near $925 — the first meaningful test of that line in this cycle. The stock is also below its 50-day average, so the shorter-term trend is soft, but the longer-term trend line is exactly where price is sitting today.

## Why it matters

This is a valuation and sentiment pullback in a company whose underlying business hasn't slowed — June comparable sales rose 8.8% and digitally-enabled sales jumped nearly 21%, both accelerations from prior trends. Costco's post-earnings weakness reflects "little room for error" positioning after a premium re-rating (shares still carry a high-40s trailing P/E) rather than any breakdown in the business, alongside overhang from a protein-powder heavy-metals lawsuit and competitive chatter around Walmart and Kroger's own membership pushes. For a mega-cap of Costco's quality, a genuine test of the 200-day average with fundamentals still improving is the type of setup covered on [stocks near their 200-day moving average](/stocks-near-200-day-moving-average).

## Levels to watch

- Support: $925 (200-day MA), with deeper support near the 52-week low of $844
- Resistance: $1,004 (50-day MA), then $1,077 (average analyst target)
- Moving averages: Price is testing the 200-day average from above after breaking below the 50-day average in recent weeks
- Risk point: A sustained daily close below $925 would break the long-term trend and open a path toward the $844 low

## What would confirm the idea

A bounce off the 200-day average with rising volume, followed by a reclaim of the 50-day average near $1,004, would confirm long-term buyers are stepping in at trend support rather than the stock cracking through it.

## What would weaken the idea

A daily close below $925 that doesn't recover within a few sessions, especially alongside any further deceleration in comp sales, would signal the pullback is turning into a deeper trend break rather than a routine test.

## Bull vs bear scenarios

**Bullish scenario:**  
Costco holds the 200-day average, comp sales momentum continues into the next earnings cycle, and shares grind back toward the analyst consensus target near $1,077 as valuation concerns fade.

**Bearish scenario:**  
The 200-day average fails to hold, valuation compression continues as investors rotate out of premium multiples, and COST drifts toward the $844 52-week low before finding real demand.

## Bottom line

Costco is doing exactly what a high-quality mega-cap does in a valuation reset: pulling back to its long-term trend line while the actual business keeps accelerating. The 200-day average is the line in the sand — holding it keeps the long-term uptrend intact, losing it says the market wants a cheaper entry.
