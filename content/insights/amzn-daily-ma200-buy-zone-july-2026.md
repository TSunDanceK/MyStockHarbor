## What happened

Amazon has pulled back from its 52-week high - down roughly 11% - and is now trading just above its rising 200-day moving average, a level it hasn't tested from above in some time. The stock has already broken below its 20-day and 50-day averages, so the near-term trend is soft, but the longer-term uptrend structure is still intact as long as the 200-day holds.

## Why it matters

This pullback is macro and sector-led, not company-specific. Alphabet raised its 2026 capital spending plan to as high as $205 billion this week, alongside a concerning detail: that guidance came with negative free cash flow. Tesla also disclosed capex above $25 billion for the year. Both announcements spooked the market into pricing in the risk that Amazon - which already has roughly $200 billion in planned 2026 capex - could raise its own spending guidance when it reports Q2 earnings on July 30. That worry has some basis: Amazon's trailing twelve-month free cash flow has already collapsed to $1.2 billion from $25.9 billion a year earlier as infrastructure spending has ramped. The counterweight is that AWS grew revenue 28% year-over-year last quarter, its fastest growth in 15 quarters, which is the clearest evidence that the spending is translating into results rather than just weighing on the balance sheet. For readers screening large caps sitting near this specific technical level, this setup lines up with our [stocks near the 200-day moving average](/stocks-near-200-day-moving-average) screen.

## Levels to watch

- Support: 200-day moving average (current test level), with $225 as the next level down if that breaks
- Resistance: $249.50, the 50-day moving average AMZN would need to reclaim
- Moving averages: Price is below the 20-day and 50-day but still above the rising 200-day
- Risk point: A decisive daily close below the 200-day moving average and below $225

## What would confirm the idea

A hold above the 200-day moving average into and through the July 30 report, followed by a reclaim of the 50-day average near $249.50, would confirm buyers are defending the level. Q2 results showing continued AWS growth without an alarming jump in capex guidance would reinforce the case.

## What would weaken the idea

A decisive close below the 200-day moving average, especially one that also breaks $225, would invalidate the buy-zone thesis and suggest the pullback has further to run. A capex guidance raise that isn't matched by AWS growth acceleration would also undercut the setup.

## Bull vs bear scenarios

**Bullish scenario:**  
AMZN holds the 200-day moving average, Q2 earnings show AWS growth holding near recent highs without a scary capex surprise, and the stock reclaims $249.50 on its way back toward the 52-week high.

**Bearish scenario:**  
Amazon raises capex guidance meaningfully, free cash flow concerns dominate the earnings reaction, the 200-day moving average breaks, and the stock slides toward $225 and below.

## Bottom line

AMZN is at a genuine decision point - testing long-term trend support two trading days before the report that will most likely decide which way it breaks. The 200-day moving average is the level that matters here, not just proximity to it.
