## What's happening

Archer Aviation is building Midnight, an electric air taxi, and says it was the first eVTOL company to close Phase 3 of the FAA's four-phase type-certification process, in April 2026. It is now in Phase 4, the formal testing stage. Outside the US, the UAE's aviation regulator moved Midnight onto a restricted type-certificate track in May, which may allow a launch in Abu Dhabi before full FAA approval, though Archer has not given a date and regional airspace has been disrupted since late February.

The bigger recent change is corporate. In August Archer agreed to acquire three Boeing businesses: Wisk (autonomous air taxis), Insitu (military drones) and SkyGrid (airspace software). The US antitrust waiting period expired on 18 September, and Archer is aiming to close by the end of 2026. Financial terms have not been disclosed.

## Why it matters

Archer has not sold an aircraft yet. In Q2 2026 it reported $5.0M of revenue, mostly fuel, ground handling and leases at Hawthorne Airport, where it took a 75% stake in April, against a $263.2M net loss. It held $1.56bn of cash and short-term investments at 30 June, down $215.3M from the end of March, and its share count was about 20% higher than a year earlier.

If the Boeing deal closes, Insitu's existing drone business may change Archer's revenue picture well before air-taxi flights do; Archer has put Insitu's revenue at over $200M a year. That could make Archer look less like a single-product start-up, but it also brings integration risk. For who Archer relies on to build Midnight, see the [Archer bottleneck map](/bottlenecks/achr).

## What to watch next

- **FAA Phase 4:** progress in formal testing may set the pace for any US launch.
- **UAE:** a confirmed Abu Dhabi launch date might bring the first passenger revenue.
- **The Boeing deal:** closing, and any disclosed terms, may reshape the revenue mix.
- **Stellantis:** Archer's 10-K says the final manufacturing terms are still uncertain.
- **The Q3 report:** not yet dated; one estimate puts it around 5 November.

## Bottom line

Archer is moving through the last FAA phase with a planned UAE launch and a deal that could add a real revenue stream. It is also spending heavily and diluting shareholders to get there. How those pieces come together may matter more over the coming months than any single headline.
