## What happened

DIS has fallen roughly 14-17% year-to-date in 2026 and is now trading in the mid-$90s, within a few percent of its 52-week low near $92 and firmly below its 200-day moving average. Rather than a single-day shock, this is the tail end of a grinding multi-month downtrend that has pulled a mega-cap, S&P 500 constituent down toward a level where its long-term average now acts as overhead resistance rather than support.

## Why it matters

Disney's decline has been driven mainly by investor skepticism that hasn't kept pace with operational improvement: the theme parks and experiences division grew 7% year-over-year under new CEO Josh D'Amaro's parks-first approach, the streaming business is approaching profitability, and Toy Story 5 gave the studio a rare box-office win heading into summer. That combination of real fundamental progress against a share price still sitting near 52-week lows is the classic setup for a large-cap value gap - the market hasn't yet re-rated the stock even though the underlying story has improved. This is a mega-cap name, so the move is driven far more by sentiment and sector rotation in media stocks than by any single catalyst, which is exactly why the 200-day moving average test matters here - it is one of the cleanest reads on whether institutional money is willing to defend this level. It's also a good match for names showing up on our [stocks near their 200-day moving average](/stocks-near-200-day-moving-average) screen.

## Levels to watch

- Support: $92-$94 (52-week low zone)
- Resistance: 200-day moving average (currently roughly $105-$107), then $110-$115
- Moving averages: Price is running below its 200-day MA, which now caps rallies until reclaimed
- Risk point: A daily close below $92 breaks the 52-week low and invalidates the buy-zone thesis

## What would confirm the idea

A rally that closes back above the 200-day moving average on strong volume - ideally into or following the early-August earnings report - would confirm the buy zone is holding and a durable base has formed. Guidance that reiterates or raises the 12% EPS growth target would add fundamental confirmation.

## What would weaken the idea

A failed rally that gets rejected at the 200-day average and rolls back toward $92 would weaken the setup, as would an earnings report that misses on parks growth or streaming margins. A break of the 52-week low would signal the downtrend is still in control.

## Bull vs bear scenarios

**Bullish scenario:**  
DIS holds above $92, reclaims its 200-day moving average into the August earnings date, and follows through toward $115-$120 as parks and streaming progress gets re-rated.

**Bearish scenario:**  
Earnings disappoint or macro pressure on media/entertainment names persists, DIS loses the $92 floor, and the stock resets toward a new multi-year low.

## Bottom line

Disney is a rare setup: a household-name mega-cap trading near 52-week lows below its 200-day average, with real operational improvement the market hasn't priced in yet. August earnings is the decision point.
