## What happened

AeroVironment has suffered one of the sharper collapses in the defense tech sector this year. The stock peaked above $400 in late 2025 and has since fallen more than 60% to trade near its 52-week low around $170, with the decline arriving in three distinct legs rather than a single crash. The first drop came on January 20, 2026, when the U.S. Space Force issued a stop-work order on the company's BADGER antenna systems contract for the SCAR program — a $1.7 billion deal that had been acquired through the BlueHalo purchase. Shares fell nearly 16% that day. A second 17% decline followed on March 2 after SpaceNews reported the Space Force was reopening the SCAR program under a new acquisition strategy. Then on March 10, the company reported a Q3 operating loss of $179 million — driven by a $151 million goodwill impairment tied to the contract — sending shares down another 6%. In total, those three disclosures accounted for roughly 40% of the decline from peak, with the rest coming from ongoing litigation overhang and broader sentiment.

## Why it matters

The SCAR contract loss matters beyond the direct revenue impact because it exposed a concentration risk that investors hadn't fully priced in. BlueHalo was acquired partly on the strength of that $1.7B Space Force programme, and when the programme was pulled the associated goodwill had to be written down in a single quarter, turning what had been a profitable period into a large reported loss. The underlying autonomous systems business — small UAS, loitering munitions, counter-drone systems — has continued to grow. AeroVironment was awarded a $117 million Army contract in June 2026, secured a LASSO prototype agreement for counter-UAS from the U.S. Army, and carries a record $4.6 billion backlog. Analysts still hold a consensus Buy with average price targets well above current prices. But the securities lawsuit filed in June, which alleges executives misrepresented the stability of the SCAR contract across a nine-month class period, is keeping fresh selling pressure on the stock as the July 27 lead-plaintiff deadline approaches.
