## What happened

Texas Instruments has fallen roughly 13% from its June high near $332 to current levels around $288, sliding below its 50-day and 100-day moving averages in the process and arriving right at its rising 200-day moving average. That's a textbook long-term support test after a sharp pullback, not a stock breaking down through key structure.

## Why it matters

Texas Instruments is one of the most closely watched analog-chip bellwethers in the S&P 500, and this pullback has come alongside a broader semiconductor-sector correction rather than any company-specific setback — no negative company news has accompanied the decline. That distinction matters: several analysts have continued raising price targets into the $340–390 range even as the stock fell, suggesting the move is being read as valuation digestion after a strong run rather than a change in the fundamental picture. The setup lines up with the broader group of [large-caps testing their 200-day moving average](/stocks-near-200-day-moving-average) right now, and TXN's timing is sharper than most — it reports Q2 earnings on Wednesday, right into this test of long-term support.

## Levels to watch

- Support: 200-day moving average, roughly $295–300
- Resistance: $310–315 near-term supply, then the June high near $332
- Moving averages: Price has slipped below both the 50-day and 100-day averages and is now testing the longer-term 200-day from just underneath
- Risk point: A sustained daily close below $280 that also breaks the 200-day MA

## What would confirm the idea

A hold of the 200-day average into earnings, followed by an earnings reaction that reclaims the 50-day and 100-day averages, would confirm buyers are defending long-term support. Given TXN's recent track record of beating revenue estimates and management's own commentary pointing toward accelerating free cash flow, a clean beat-and-hold reaction would go a long way toward confirming the buy-zone thesis.

## What would weaken the idea

A decisive close below $280 — particularly a post-earnings gap down through the 200-day average — would flip this from a support test into a genuine breakdown, especially if it came alongside guidance that contradicts the recent industrial and data-center demand recovery story.

## Bull vs bear scenarios

**Bullish scenario:**  
TXN holds the 200-day average through earnings, delivers another beat consistent with its recent track record, and reclaims the 50-day and 100-day averages on the way back toward the June high near $332.

**Bearish scenario:**  
Earnings disappoint or guidance undercuts the demand-recovery narrative, TXN breaks decisively below $280 and the 200-day average, and the correction extends toward the next support levels below.

## Bottom line

Texas Instruments is testing a genuine long-term support level heading into a binary earnings catalyst — a setup worth watching closely rather than a stock in a clean uptrend or an outright breakdown.
