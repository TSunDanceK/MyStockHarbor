## What happened

Micron Technology (MU) has pulled back sharply from its June 25 all-time high near $1,255, now trading in the $950-1,030 range — a roughly 20% correction that has broken the parabolic breakout shelf flagged in early June near $1,040-1,050.

The stock is now testing its daily MA50, turning what was a pure momentum breakout into a genuine trend-support question for the first time since the AI memory rally began.

## Why it matters

Micron's fiscal Q3 results, reported June 24, were genuinely record-setting: revenue nearly quadrupled year over year to $41.46 billion, EPS beat estimates by more than 24%, and management guided toward another record quarter. That's not the profile of a stock breaking down on fundamentals.

Instead, the pullback lines up with two separate pressures. First, memory-supply-glut fears have periodically hit the stock even as the company signs multi-year customer agreements (most recently with General Motors on July 1) that lock in demand visibility. Second, Micron, Samsung and SK Hynix are now facing a class-action lawsuit alleging DRAM price coordination — an unproven legal claim, but real headline risk layered on top of an already highly valued stock after a 300%+ year-to-date run.

## Levels to watch

- Support: $950-970, today's intraday low zone
- Resistance: $1,040-1,050, the prior breakout shelf that is now the level to reclaim
- Moving averages: Daily MA50 is the primary trend reference for this pullback
- Risk point: A daily close below $950 would suggest the correction is deepening rather than stabilizing

## What would confirm the idea

A hold above the daily MA50 and the $950-970 zone, followed by a reclaim of the $1,040-1,050 former breakout shelf on strong volume, would confirm buyers are still defending the AI memory re-rating after the recent pullback.

## What would weaken the idea

A daily close below $950, or a further escalation of the DRAM price-fixing lawsuit into something with real financial exposure, would suggest the pullback is turning into a deeper reset rather than a healthy digestion of the prior rally.

## Bull vs bear scenarios

**Bullish scenario:**  
MU holds its daily MA50, absorbs the supply-glut and lawsuit headlines, and reclaims $1,040-1,050 as the GM agreement and other multi-year deals reinforce the demand story.

**Bearish scenario:**  
MU loses the daily MA50 and $950 support, and the combination of glut fears and legal overhang forces a deeper valuation reset even as the underlying earnings story stays strong.

## Bottom line

Micron's fundamentals haven't changed — this is a valuation and sentiment pullback layered on top of record results, and the daily MA50 is now the level that decides whether the AI memory re-rating holds or needs a deeper reset.
