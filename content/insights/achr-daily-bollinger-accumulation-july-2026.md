## What happened

Archer Aviation has gone from a steady slide to a sideways grind. After dropping roughly 50% off its 52-week high, the eVTOL air-taxi name hit fresh 52-week lows near $4.58–$4.79 in late June, then stopped falling. For roughly a month now, price has bounced repeatedly between the mid-$4.70s and the $5.30–$5.60 zone without breaking either side decisively. That's a Bollinger(20,2) band tightening around price, not a stock in a clean uptrend or a single-day spike — it's a base being built.

## Why it matters

Archer is one of the most heavily traded retail names in the eVTOL space, alongside names like Joby, and sentiment there tends to move in waves — "when eVTOL names are in favor, these levels can squeeze fast," but enthusiasm cools just as quickly when certification timelines or cash-burn headlines resurface. That's exactly the dynamic showing up here: no single company-specific news event is driving the current range, this is a sector-sentiment and positioning story. Retail traders are testing the level repeatedly rather than committing size in either direction, which is typical behavior during accumulation — sellers who bought higher are exhausted, but buyers haven't yet found conviction to force a breakout. The company's roughly $951 million cash position gives it room to survive the pre-commercialization period without an immediate liquidity scare, which is likely part of what's allowing the stock to stabilize instead of continuing to break down. For traders scanning for similar setups, names that have been cut in half and are now stabilizing show up on a [stocks down 20% from all-time highs](/stocks-down-20-from-all-time-highs) screen.

## Levels to watch

- Support: $4.70–$4.80 (tested multiple times this month), secondary support near $4.50
- Resistance: $5.00, then $5.30 — described by traders as "stubborn" on repeated tests
- Moving averages: 20-day SMA (the Bollinger midline) sits around the middle of the range and is currently capping bounces
- Risk point: a sustained daily close below $4.50 would break the base and open the door back toward the 52-week low

## What would confirm the idea

A push through $5.30 on rising volume would be the first real signal that demand is starting to overwhelm supply rather than just testing it. Ideally that breakout would also come with the Bollinger bands starting to widen again after weeks of compression — a classic signal that a basing period is transitioning into a directional move. Multiple closes above $5.00 without an immediate fade would also add confidence that the range is shifting higher.

## What would weaken the idea

A break of the mid-$4.70s support with follow-through selling, rather than another bounce, would suggest supply is still in control. If price revisits and takes out the $4.50 secondary support, that would effectively invalidate the basing thesis and put the 52-week low back in play. Shrinking volume on bounces toward resistance, rather than on pullbacks, would also be a warning sign that buyers are losing interest.

## Bull vs bear scenarios

**Bullish scenario:**  
ACHR holds the $4.70–$4.80 zone on the next test, volume builds on a push through $5.00–$5.30, and the Bollinger bands begin expanding to the upside as the stock transitions out of its base into a new leg higher, consistent with renewed sector-wide eVTOL enthusiasm.

**Bearish scenario:**  
Certification or funding headlines resurface, support at $4.70 fails to hold, and the stock grinds back down toward the $4.50 level and ultimately retests the 52-week low, confirming the range was distribution rather than accumulation.

## Bottom line

ACHR has stopped bleeding and is now coiling in a defined range — the textbook setup traders watch for after a steep decline. The decision point is simple: hold $4.70 and clear $5.30 to confirm a real base, or lose $4.50 and confirm the downtrend isn't finished.
