## What happened

Boeing has spent the past several weeks working its way back up toward its 200-day moving average after trading below all four major moving averages in mid-July. Shares slipped as low as roughly $205.90 — about 5.7% under the 200-day line — before a delivery-driven bounce brought price back to around $209.52, now sitting within a few percent of the 200-day average near the $218–$220 area. That's a genuine test of long-term trend support, not a stock already miles into a new uptrend or one still in freefall.

## Why it matters

Boeing is a household name and one of the most closely watched industrials in the market, so a 200-day test here carries weight beyond the stock itself. The move back toward that line isn't happening in a vacuum: the FAA approved an increased 737 MAX production rate, removing a production-cap overhang that has weighed on the delivery ramp and cash-flow story for years, and Q2 deliveries came in strong at 171 commercial jets (129 737s, 25 787s, plus 767s and 777s), pushing year-to-date deliveries to 314 units — Boeing's best second quarter since 2018. Defense deliveries were solid too, with 35 aircraft and systems including Apache, Chinook, F-15, F/A-18 and KC-46 units, and elevated allied defense budgets provide a further tailwind on that side of the business. This is a fundamentally-supported test of trend support rather than a random bounce, and it's the kind of setup that shows up on a [stocks near 200-day moving average](/stocks-near-200-day-moving-average) screen. Analyst sentiment backs the case too: consensus rating sits at Buy with an average price target of $276.80, well above current levels, with recent target raises from Citigroup ($260) and Tigress Financial ($295).

## Levels to watch

- Support: $205–$206 (mid-July low), with the $200 level as psychological backup support
- Resistance: the 200-day moving average itself, roughly $218–$220, followed by the $230s
- Moving averages: price is testing the 200-day MA from below, with the 20-day, 50-day and 100-day averages also still overhead
- Risk point: a sustained daily close back below $200 would suggest the bounce has failed and the broader downtrend is reasserting itself

## What would confirm the idea

A daily close above the 200-day moving average, followed by the average acting as support on a retest, would be the clearest confirmation that this is a genuine trend-repair move rather than a temporary bounce. Strong follow-through around the July 28 earnings report — particularly confirmation of the delivery cadence and narrowed losses — would add real weight to the bullish case. Rising volume on the push toward $220 versus fading volume on pullbacks would also support the thesis.

## What would weaken the idea

A rejection at the 200-day line with price sliding back toward $205 and then $200 would suggest sellers are still in control at this level. A disappointing earnings report on July 28 — a wider-than-expected loss, delivery guidance cuts, or fresh production issues — would undercut the fundamental support for the bounce and could send the stock back toward its multi-month lows. Failure to hold $200 on a retest would be the clearest sign the setup has failed.

## Bottom line

Boeing is at a real decision point: a legitimate test of its 200-day moving average, backed by an actual production-rate unlock and a strong delivery quarter rather than just proximity to a line on a chart. A hold-and-reclaim into and through the July 28 earnings report would confirm the buy-zone thesis; a rejection back below $200 would put it back in question.
