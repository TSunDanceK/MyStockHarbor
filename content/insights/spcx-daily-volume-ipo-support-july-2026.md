## What happened

SpaceX (SPCX) began trading on June 12, 2026 after pricing its IPO at $135 and closing its first session at $160.95, a 19% first-day gain that valued the company at roughly $2.1 trillion. The stock kept climbing to an all-time high near $225.64 on June 16, before reversing hard — shares are now down close to 30% from that high and trading back near $150, the level of the stock's opening print on day one.

Because SPCX has only a few weeks of public trading history, this isn't a classic moving-average setup. There isn't enough data yet for a meaningful 50- or 200-day average, so this chart focuses on raw price structure and volume instead of the trend overlays used for more established names — a deliberate departure from the standard setup rules given how new the listing is.

## Why it matters

SpaceX's business spans reusable rockets, the Starlink satellite broadband network, and a growing AI/X platform segment, all of which drove enormous demand into the IPO — the deal was more than two times oversubscribed with roughly $150 billion in orders for a $75 billion raise.

The move since listing has been almost entirely sentiment- and positioning-driven rather than news- or earnings-driven, since the company has not yet reported as a public entity. The stock rallied hard into its first days of trading, likely on scarcity (only about 4% of shares are public) and momentum buying, and has since given back a large portion of that premium as early positioning unwinds. Some analysts, including Morningstar, questioned the IPO valuation well before the debut, and that skepticism appears to be resurfacing now that the initial pop has faded.

Given the lack of trading history, this setup fits best with the general [stock pickers](/pickers) page rather than any specific technical screener, since SPCX doesn't yet have the track record needed for trend- or momentum-based lists.

## Levels to watch

- Support: $150, the stock's opening print from day one of trading and a level with some psychological significance
- Resistance: $170-180, the recent congestion zone from failed bounce attempts since the June 16 high
- Moving averages: Not yet meaningful given the stock's limited trading history; price structure and volume are the primary reference points for now
- Risk point: A sustained close below $150 would put the $135 IPO price, and the stock's all-time low, back in play

## What would confirm the idea

A daily close back above the $170-180 zone on strong volume would suggest buyers are stepping back in after the post-IPO reset, and would be the first real sign the decline from the June 16 high is stabilizing.

## What would weaken the idea

A decisive break below $150 on heavy volume, especially if short sellers continue building positions as reported, would suggest the post-debut decline has further to run toward the $135 IPO price.

## Bull vs bear scenarios

**Bullish scenario:**  
SPCX holds $150, stabilizes, and starts building a base as early volatility settles, with buyers treating the opening print as a legitimate value level for a company of SpaceX's scale.

**Bearish scenario:**  
SPCX loses $150, short interest keeps building, and the stock grinds back toward its $135 IPO price as the market continues to digest the gap between IPO-week euphoria and where the stock should trade longer-term.

## Bottom line

SPCX is still finding its feet as a public stock, and the $150 opening-print level is the clearest reference point available with only a few weeks of trading history — hold it, and the post-IPO reset looks like a normal cool-down; lose it, and the $135 IPO price becomes the next real test.
