## What happened

WTI crude fell roughly 21% in May-June 2026, dragging Chevron down from the high-$180s into the mid-$160s by late June — a slide that pushed shares right down to test the Daily 200-day moving average near $172. Rather than breaking down further, CVX held that zone, bottoming near $165-168, and has since staged a sharp reversal back to $187.38 as of July 17, roughly 8.5% back above the 200-day MA.

## Why it matters

This is a textbook buy-zone test: a mega-cap, Dow-component energy name selling off with the broader crude complex rather than on company-specific weakness, finding support exactly where technicians would expect it — the long-term trend average — and then reversing. The move lines up with a wave of analyst upgrades: Wolfe Research moved to Outperform with a $210 target, explicitly citing an attractive entry point after the pullback, while RBC and UBS both reiterated Buy ratings with $220 targets. Underneath the technical bounce sits real fundamental support: Chevron's Hess-integrated production jumped 15% to 3,858 MBOED, Permian output topped 1 million barrels a day, and the company is diversifying beyond pure oil-and-gas with its Kilby power project, a 2.67-GW facility contracted to supply Microsoft data centers for 20 years. ## Levels to watch

- Support: 200-day MA near $172, with a secondary floor at the June low around $165
- Resistance: 52-week high at $214.71
- Moving averages: price is now trading roughly 8.5% above the 200-day MA, confirming the reclaim
- Risk point: a daily close back below the 200-day MA (~$172) would put the reclaim in doubt and reopen the June lows

## What would confirm the idea

Continued closes above the 200-day MA, ideally with the average itself flattening or turning back up, would confirm the base held and the pullback was a genuine buying opportunity rather than the start of a bigger breakdown.

## What would weaken the idea

A fresh leg down in crude oil prices that drags CVX back under $172 and toward the June lows would undercut the reclaim and suggest the bounce was just a relief rally within a larger downtrend.

## Bull vs bear scenarios

**Bullish scenario:**  
CVX holds above the 200-day MA, oil prices stabilize in line with EIA's $79-$106 Brent guidance, and shares grind back toward the 52-week high near $214 as Hess-driven production growth and the Kilby power deal support a re-rating.

**Bearish scenario:**  
Oil weakness resumes, crude breaks toward new lows, and CVX slips back under the 200-day MA, testing the June low near $165 and raising fresh questions about dividend coverage if free cash flow stays pressured.

## Bottom line

Chevron's pullback to its 200-day moving average looks like a textbook buy-zone test that's already been reclaimed, backed by real production growth and a string of analyst upgrades — the level to watch now is whether $172 holds as support on the next dip.
