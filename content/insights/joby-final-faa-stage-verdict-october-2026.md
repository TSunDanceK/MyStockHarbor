## What's happening

Joby Aviation is trying to become one of the first companies to carry paying passengers in an electric air taxi. It says it is in the fifth and final stage of the FAA's type-certification process: its first FAA-conforming aircraft has been flying since March 2026, and the formal "for-credit" flights with FAA pilots are expected later this year. In Dubai, where Joby holds a six-year exclusive right to operate air taxis, first passengers were targeted for later in 2026, though no firm date has been given.

The newest development cuts the other way. On about 2 October a federal jury in Tampa awarded Aerosonic, a former supplier of air-data probes for Joby's test aircraft, $116.9M in a trade-secret case. Joby says it developed its own air-data system and plans to seek post-trial and appeal remedies, so the final cost, if any, may be some way off.

## Why it matters

Joby is still pre-commercial on the air-taxi side, so its filings show a company spending heavily ahead of revenue. In Q2 2026 it reported a $245.4M net loss on $38.6M of revenue, almost all of which ($36.2M) came from Blade, the helicopter and jet passenger business it bought in August 2025. It held $2.26bn of cash and short-term investments at 30 June, after raising roughly $1.3bn in January through new shares and convertible notes. The weighted share count in Q2 was about 22% higher than a year earlier, so existing holders have been diluted to fund the build-out.

That cash cushion is what gives the certification timeline room to run, but it also means the verdict, any further raises and the pace of spending may all be read against how much runway is left. For who Joby relies on to build and fly its aircraft, see the [Joby bottleneck map](/bottlenecks/joby).

## What to watch next

- **FAA for-credit flights:** progress here may be the clearest sign of how close certification is.
- **Dubai:** a confirmed first-passenger date might shift attention from cost to revenue.
- **The Aerosonic case:** post-trial motions and any appeal may change the size and timing of the award.
- **Toyota:** a further $250M investment is expected in late 2026 or early 2027, once supply agreements are final.
- **The Q3 report:** not yet dated; one estimate puts it around 4 November.

## Bottom line

Joby looks to be near the end of a long certification road, with a large cash buffer and a launch market lined up. Against that sit heavy losses, steady dilution and a new legal bill that may or may not stick. How those balance over the next few months may depend more on the FAA and Dubai timelines than on anything in a single quarter's numbers.
