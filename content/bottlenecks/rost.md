summary: >
  Ross Stores runs the country's largest off-price retail chain - Ross Dress
  for Less and dd's DISCOUNTS - built entirely around opportunistic buying
  rather than long-term vendor contracts, so it has no material single-vendor
  dependency on the supply side. Its buyers are simply the tens of millions
  of walk-in shoppers across more than 2,180 US stores; with no membership
  program or loyalty tier to create customer concentration, the more
  meaningful and genuinely disclosed concentration story is which merchandise
  categories - led by home goods, not apparel - actually drive its sales.
supplyChainNote: >
  Ross Stores' own 10-K describes "a large network of merchandise vendors
  and manufacturers" - more than 800 merchants across Ross and dd's
  DISCOUNTS combined - with no long-term purchase contracts and no material
  dependence on any single vendor. Merchandise is acquired opportunistically
  from manufacturer and brand overruns, canceled orders, and off-season
  "packaway" buys, so a literal vendor-concentration chart isn't something
  Ross discloses. The named companies below are large, publicly known
  apparel and footwear brands widely reported to move excess inventory
  through off-price channels like Ross; percentages are editorial estimates
  of relative importance to that closeout pipeline, not a vendor-level
  purchase breakdown Ross itself publishes.
customersNote: >
  Ross has no concentrated corporate or membership customer base - unlike

  walk-in shoppers across its US-only store base. The most meaningful
  concentration Ross actually discloses is by merchandise category: which
  parts of the store - home goods, ladies' apparel, men's, and so on - make
  up its sales mix, per its own reported fiscal 2024 department breakdown.
