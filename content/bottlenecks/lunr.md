Intuitive Machines became the first commercial company to land (however
roughly) on the Moon, but its bottleneck story has two very different
halves. On the supply side, it's unusually self-reliant for a hardware
company — it builds its own engine, structures, and avionics — leaving
SpaceX's launch pad as the one dependency it genuinely cannot work around,
plus a handful of named partners on NASA's Lunar Terrain Vehicle program. On
the customer side, the picture is the opposite of diversified: NASA has been
the company's primary source of revenue since before its 2023 IPO, and while
2026 brought real diversification — a Space Development Agency subcontract
via L3Harris, an Air Force lunar-power award, a Korean government imaging
contract, and paying commercial payload customers like Nokia and Lonestar —
none of it yet approaches the scale of the core NASA relationship.
