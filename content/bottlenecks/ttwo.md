summary: >
  Take-Two is the parent of Rockstar Games (Grand Theft Auto, Red Dead
  Redemption), 2K (NBA 2K, Borderlands), and mobile studio Zynga (Toon Blast,
  Words With Friends). Its supply-chain story is dominated by the console
  platform holders that certify and control manufacturing of every physical
  and digital release, the cloud infrastructure that keeps Zynga's live
  mobile games running, and a small number of licensed technologies and IP
  deals — like Unreal Engine and the NBA license — that specific franchises
  can't do without. Because Take-Two sells to millions of individual
  consumers rather than a handful of corporate buyers, its "customers" chart
  is reframed around the storefront and platform gatekeepers that Take-Two's
  own SEC filings identify as its largest revenue counterparties.
customersNote: >
  Take-Two does not sell to a concentrated base of corporate customers — its
  games are purchased by tens of millions of individual players worldwide.
  However, its fiscal year 2026 10-K discloses that sales to its five
  largest customers accounted for 80.6% of net revenue, with Apple, Sony,
  Google, and Microsoft each individually exceeding 10% of net revenue. This
  reflects how digital storefronts and console platform holders act as the
  merchant of record and collect payment on Take-Two's behalf, creating real
  distribution and revenue-recognition concentration even though no single
  end consumer represents a meaningful share of sales. The chart below
  reframes "customers" around this platform/storefront concentration rather
  than literal end-buyer revenue share.
