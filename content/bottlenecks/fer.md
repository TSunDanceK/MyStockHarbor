summary: >
  Ferrovial is a Netherlands-based global infrastructure group - traded on
  Nasdaq, Euronext Amsterdam, and the Spanish exchange, and a Nasdaq-100
  constituent since December 2025 - built around three core businesses:
  construction (through Webber in the US and its majority-owned Polish
  subsidiary Budimex), toll-road concessions (through Cintra, including a
  controlling stake in Toronto's 407 ETR), and a shrinking airport
  footprint after fully exiting both Heathrow and AGS Airports in 2025.
  Because Ferrovial doesn't manufacture anything and has no ordinary
  corporate customers, both charts below are reframed: the supply-chain
  side shows the co-investors, contractors, and tolling-technology vendors
  it depends on for its biggest projects, and the customers side shows its
  actual segment revenue mix rather than named accounts.
supplyChainNote: >
  Ferrovial isn't a manufacturer, so it has no conventional parts-and-
  materials supply chain. The chart below instead shows the co-investors,
  design-build contractors, and specialized tolling-technology vendors
  Ferrovial depends on to build and operate its largest concessions and
  projects, including its 407 ETR toll road, its Cintra managed-lane
  network, and its JFK New Terminal One stake - relationships that
  function as real operational bottlenecks even though they aren't
  suppliers in the traditional sense.
customersNote: >
  Ferrovial has no corporate customers in the usual sense: its toll roads
  are used by millions of individual drivers, and its construction
  contracts run through public agencies with no disclosed per-counterparty
  revenue share. The chart below instead uses Ferrovial's actual FY2025
  segment revenue mix - construction, toll roads/highways, and energy - as
  the most honest available proxy for where its revenue depends most
  heavily, the same approach used for freight railroads that don't
  disclose shipper-level concentration. Airports no longer appear as a
  revenue segment because Ferrovial fully exited both Heathrow and AGS
  Airports in 2025; its remaining airport exposure (a stake in JFK's New
  Terminal One) is accounted for as an equity investment, not consolidated
  revenue.
