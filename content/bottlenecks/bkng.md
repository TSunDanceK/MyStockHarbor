summary: >
  Booking Holdings runs Booking.com, Priceline, Agoda, Kayak, OpenTable and
  Rentalcars.com, stitching together millions of independent hotels, cloud
  infrastructure, payment rails and airline content into one travel
  marketplace. Its supply chain runs through AWS for cloud and AI
  infrastructure, Amadeus for flight content and GDS distribution, and Adyen
  and the card networks for global payments. On the demand side, Booking is
  a two-sided marketplace with no single concentrated paying customer, so
  its real concentration risk sits with the channels that bring travelers to
  its sites and apps in the first place, led heavily by Google.
customersNote: >
  Booking Holdings doesn't have customer concentration in the traditional
  sense: its revenue comes from commissions paid by hundreds of thousands of
  independent hotels, alternative-accommodation hosts and travel suppliers
  worldwide, and no single property or chain accounts for a material share
  of bookings. The real concentration risk instead sits with the
  demand-generation and distribution channels that funnel travelers onto
  Booking's platforms - most notably Google, which analysts estimate absorbs
  well over half of Booking's multibillion-dollar annual performance
  marketing budget through search ads and organic search visibility, plus
  app stores, social platforms and metasearch sites, and the large branded
  hotel chains that increasingly push guests toward direct bookings. The
  entries below represent these channel and distribution dependencies rather
  than literal paying customers.
