summary: >
  Karman Space & Defense builds the mission-critical hardware that sits between a
  rocket's stages and around its payload: fairings, nose cones, shrouds, interstages,
  separation systems, solid rocket motor cases and nozzles, and launch canisters. It
  is a subsystems supplier rather than a prime, selling into more than 80 customers
  and 130-plus programs across space and launch, hypersonics and strategic missile
  defense, tactical missiles, and — since the January 2026 Seemann Composites
  acquisition — maritime defense. Its dependency profile is unusual in both
  directions: Karman is heavily vertically integrated, so its upstream exposure is to
  raw material families (aerospace composites, specialty and refractory alloys,
  energetic materials) rather than to named component vendors, while downstream it
  discloses that three unnamed customers made up 51.5% of fiscal 2025 revenue.
supplyChainNote: >
  Karman does not name a single supplier in its public filings, and it manufactures
  most of its own hardware in-house — machining, composite layup, and refractory
  alloy work all sit inside the company. What it does disclose is the raw material
  families it depends on (composite prepregs including epoxy, BMI and phenolic
  systems; aluminum, copper and specialty alloys; refractory metals; specialty
  chemicals; energetic materials) and that one unnamed supplier accounted for 23.8%
  of its accounts payable at 31 December 2025. The chart below is therefore built
  around the dominant merchant suppliers of those disclosed input categories, sized
  by how critical and how hard to substitute each input is — it is an editorial map
  of material dependency, not a list of confirmed vendor relationships.
customersNote: >
  Karman's 10-K discloses that three customers each exceeded 10% of fiscal 2025
  revenue — at 28.5%, 12.8% and 10.2%, or 51.5% combined — but does not name them,
  and notes that no single program exceeded 12% of sales. The chart below therefore
  reframes the customer story around the prime contractors, launch providers and
  government agencies Karman itself has publicly named on its own site, in press
  releases and in contract announcements, sized by an editorial estimate of program
  exposure rather than by disclosed revenue share. It should be read as a map of who
  Karman's hardware ends up flying on, not as an audited concentration table.
