summary: >
  Electronic Arts is one of the world's largest video game publishers, built
  around sports simulations (EA SPORTS FC, Madden NFL, EA SPORTS PGA TOUR,
  EA SPORTS NHL, EA SPORTS College Football), live-service titles (Apex
  Legends, Battlefield) and long-running franchises like The Sims and
  Dragon Age, generating roughly $7.5 billion in net revenue and a record
  $8.0 billion in net bookings in fiscal 2026. Two very different
  dependency stories define its risk profile: distribution gatekeepers -
  Sony, Microsoft, Valve, Apple and Google - that control access to
  console, PC and mobile players and take a cut of nearly every
  transaction; and sports/brand licensing deals, most critically an
  exclusive NFL license (extended through the 2030 season) and a
  fragmented web of football league and federation licenses EA has had to
  rebuild individually since losing the FIFA trademark in 2023. EA also
  agreed in September 2025 to be taken private by Saudi Arabia's Public
  Investment Fund, Silver Lake and Affinity Partners in a $55 billion deal
  that remained pending regulatory (CFIUS) review as of mid-2026, so EA
  still trades publicly under ticker EA for now.
supplyChainNote: >
  EA has no physical supply chain - its closest analogue is the small
  number of platform and storefront gatekeepers through which almost all
  of its console, PC and mobile revenue is collected. Sony, Microsoft,
  Valve, Apple and Google each control certification/approval and take a
  standard cut of transactions, making them functionally EA's suppliers of
  market access, the same way Apple and Google are treated as
  infrastructure dependencies (not customers) for other consumer platforms.
  Layered on top are the licensing relationships that are essential
  creative inputs to EA's largest franchises - above all the NFL for
  Madden and the patchwork of football leagues and federations behind EA
  SPORTS FC - plus a technology license (Unreal Engine) and cloud
  infrastructure (AWS) that support development and live services.
customersNote: >
  EA's real customer base is tens of millions of individual console, PC
  and mobile players worldwide, which is inherently diversified - a
  strength, not a bottleneck, much like Tesla's car buyers. Rather than
  force a misleading concentration chart around that diversified base, or
  simply repeat the same platform holders shown as distribution
  gatekeepers in the supply-chain chart above, this chart isolates the one
  genuine, separately disclosed customer-style relationship EA has: the
  subscription-catalog licensing deals under which Microsoft (Xbox Game
  Pass Ultimate) and Sony (PlayStation Plus) pay EA directly to bundle its
  EA Play catalog for subscribers - a distinct commercial arrangement from
  the transaction fees those same companies charge EA as storefront
  operators. The large remaining share below is the diversified
  individual-consumer base that still drives the majority of EA's revenue
  through direct game purchases and live-service spending.
