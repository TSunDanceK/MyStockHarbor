summary: >
  Airbnb runs a two-sided marketplace connecting more than 5 million
  individual hosts with guests worldwide, so it has no concentrated
  supplier or customer base in the traditional sense. Its real
  dependencies run through the small set of infrastructure and financial
  vendors that keep the marketplace running - cloud hosting, payment
  processors, and card networks - while on the revenue side, its most
  meaningful concentration is geographic rather than tied to any single
  customer or distribution partner.
supplyChainNote: >
  Airbnb's supply side is its more than 5 million individual hosts
  worldwide, none of whom is remotely material on their own - so a
  literal supplier-concentration chart would be meaningless. Instead,
  this page shows the critical infrastructure and financial vendors
  Airbnb's 10-K discloses reliance on: cloud hosting, payment processing
  and card networks, and app-store distribution. Percentages are
  directional estimates of relative operational criticality, not
  disclosed reliance figures - Airbnb doesn't publish vendor-level
  dependency data.
customersNote: >
  Airbnb has tens of millions of individual guest customers with no
  concentration in any single customer, corporate account, or booking
  channel - unlike hotels, it sells almost entirely direct rather than
  through OTAs or metasearch intermediaries (CEO Brian Chesky has said
  roughly 90% of Airbnb's traffic is direct). The most meaningful
  concentration that actually exists is geographic: this chart reflects
  Airbnb's regional revenue mix rather than customer entities.
