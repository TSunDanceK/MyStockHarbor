summary: >
  Alnylam Pharmaceuticals turned the Nobel Prize-winning discovery of RNA
  interference into four approved medicines — Onpattro, Givlaari, Oxlumo and
  the fast-growing Amvuttra — that silence disease-causing genes at the RNA
  level rather than targeting the proteins they produce. Its supply chain
  leans heavily on in-house drug-substance manufacturing in Massachusetts,
  backstopped by a small number of named third-party manufacturers and
  undisclosed specialty-chemical suppliers for the raw materials each siRNA
  molecule requires. On the customer side, Alnylam's own product sales are
  concentrated through a small number of large distributors, while a second
  and increasingly important pillar of revenue comes from licensing its RNAi
  platform to big pharma partners — Novartis (Leqvio), Roche (zilebesiran)
  and Regeneron — who pay Alnylam royalties and collaboration fees on
  medicines built with its technology.
