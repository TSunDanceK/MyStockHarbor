summary: >
  Honeywell spent late 2025 through mid-2026 splitting itself into three
  separate public companies: Solstice Advanced Materials (spun off October
  2025), Honeywell Aerospace (spun off June 2026, now trading as HONA), and
  the remaining "Honeywell Technologies," which kept the HON ticker as a
  pure-play automation company spanning Building Automation, Industrial
  Automation, and Process Automation & Technology. That leaves HON's supply
  chain built on electronic components, specialty materials, and precision
  parts feeding sensors, controls, and process equipment, while its customer
  base is broadly diversified across building owners, industrial OEMs,
  distributors, and energy/chemical producers rather than concentrated in a
  handful of buyers the way the old aerospace-heavy conglomerate was.
customersNote: >
  Honeywell has long disclosed that no single customer accounts for a
  material share of sales, and that diversification is now even more
  pronounced after the Aerospace and Advanced Materials businesses (and
  their Boeing/Airbus/defense concentration) were spun into separate
  companies. Rather than implying a concentrated-revenue story that doesn't
  exist, this list highlights the most notable channel partners and
  end-market relationships across Honeywell's remaining Building, Industrial,
  and Process Automation businesses.
