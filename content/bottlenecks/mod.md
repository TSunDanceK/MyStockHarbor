summary: >
  Modine Manufacturing has spent a century as a thermal-management supplier to
  truck, auto, and industrial-equipment makers, but its fastest-growing business
  today is data-center cooling — chillers, coolant distribution units, and other
  systems sold under its Airedale and CoolTech brands to hyperscale and AI
  infrastructure operators. On the supply side, Modine's own filings describe a
  deliberately diversified base of aluminum, copper, and steel suppliers rather
  than single-vendor dependence, though a real and current bottleneck has
  emerged: a fiscal 2026 shortage of data-center cooling components as demand
  outpaced supplier capacity. On the customer side, Modine discloses genuine
  concentration — its ten largest customers made up 49 percent of fiscal 2026
  net sales, including one unnamed hyperscale technology customer alone worth
  roughly 11 percent, alongside longstanding truck and equipment OEMs like
  Volkswagen's Traton brands, Volvo, Stellantis, Caterpillar, and Deere. Modine
  also plans to spin off its legacy vehicular Performance Technologies segment
  to combine with Gentherm by the end of 2026, a deal that would leave a
  pure-play climate/data-center company behind.
supplyChainNote: >
  Modine's own disclosures describe its supply-chain risk at the material and
  component-category level rather than naming individual vendor companies — it
  states it buys aluminum, copper, steel, and stainless steel from "several
  domestic and foreign suppliers," while separately warning it relies on "a
  limited number of suppliers" for certain components that can take months to
  requalify if lost. The chart below reflects those disclosed categories,
  including the newly emerging data-center component shortage flagged in
  Modine's fiscal 2026 results, rather than single corporate names Modine
  hasn't disclosed.
