summary: >
  Constellation is the largest operator of nuclear power plants in the US,
  and its fuel supply chain depends on a small set of named fabricators and
  equipment makers to keep those reactors running. Most of its output still
  sells through diversified wholesale power markets and regulated-utility
  offtake, but a wave of headline-grabbing, long-term power purchase
  agreements with major tech companies and the federal government - signed
  to feed data-center demand - has become the most strategically important
  and closely watched part of its customer base.
customersNote: >
  Constellation's 10-K discloses no single customer above 10% of revenue,
  and the large majority of its output still sells through diversified
  regulated-utility, municipal, and wholesale-market channels (chiefly PJM
  Interconnection). The chart below instead highlights the newer, smaller
  but strategically significant slice of named long-term power purchase
  agreements with major tech companies and the federal government - signed
  specifically to supply data-center and government electricity demand -
  which together still represent only a modest fraction of Constellation's
  roughly 55 GW combined generation fleet, but have driven most of the
  company's recent dealmaking and stock-market attention.
